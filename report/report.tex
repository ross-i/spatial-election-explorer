\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{see-figure-overview.png}
  \caption{The Spatial Election Explorer (SEE) synthetic data interface.
  Voters (blue points) and candidates (orange stars) are embedded in an abstract 2D election space (unitless, $[0,1]$); the interface lets the user place points manually, sample
  from synthetic distributions, load survey-grounded data, manage stacked data layers, and run an election under a chosen voting rule (\S\ref{sub:Input})(\S\ref{sec:Preliminaries}).
  \textbf{(A)} Top bar: add voters or candidates by hand, launch the tutorial, import or export the current configuration.
  \textbf{(B)} Winners are rendered at full opacity; eliminated candidates are dimmed in place.
  \textbf{(C)} Gaussian sampling distribution, drawn as concentric
  $1\sigma$/$2\sigma$/$3\sigma$ contours.
  \textbf{(D)} Tabs switch between synthetic
  (\S\ref{sub:Input}) and survey data exploration (\S\ref{sub:Survey Data}).
  \textbf{(E)} Synthetic distribution configuration. (\S\ref{sub:Input}).
  \textbf{(F)} Each manual point or sampled distribution is a stackable,
  editable layer. \textit{Pencil icon}: edit distribution settings, click update to resample points. 
  \textit{Trash icon}: delete these point(s). \textit{Eye icon}: hide this layer from the plot and election run.
  \textbf{(G)} Choose voting rule (\S\ref{sec:Preliminaries}) and number of winners, then run the election.}
  \label{fig:overview}
\end{figure*}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{see-figure-survey.png}
  \caption{The survey data interface. Voters are loaded from a real political opinion
  poll (here, the Fall 2014 Statewide IL Poll, 1006 respondents) and
  projected onto two user-chosen ideology axes --- in this example, Role of
  Government $\times$ Social Issues. Each axis is a weighted aggregate of
  several survey questions, re-rankable by the user. 
  \textbf{(A)} Axis selection and survey question ranking panel.
  \textbf{(B)} +Random candidate creates a candidate point by randomly selecting answers to all survey questions.
  \textbf{(C)} Each candidate point is a stackable layer, editing is limited to candidates that map to the survey questions.
   \textit{Pencil icon}: edit survey question responses, opens the candidate profile window.
  \textit{Trash icon}: delete the candidate \textit{Eye icon}: hide this candidate from the plot and election run.
  \textbf{(D)} Candidate profile window opens upon clicking the +Profile candidate button, allowing users to manually specify a 
  candidate's responses to the survey questions to fit an ideology profile of their choice.
  \textbf{(E)} Annotations along the axis signal to users the general ideological direction of the axis.
  }
  \label{fig:survey}
\end{figure*}
